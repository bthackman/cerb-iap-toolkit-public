# IAP / CERB Process Reference

Source: Internship in Architecture Program (IAP) Manual, 4th Edition, July 1 2020 (revised July 1 2022).
Published by ROAC — Regulatory Organizations of Architecture in Canada (formerly CALA in older
editions; older sources may say "CALA jurisdiction" where this edition says "ROAC jurisdiction" — same
meaning).

This is the standard pathway that applies to **every** Intern Architect in Canada, including Syllabus
students (see `syllabus-vs-standard.md` for the syllabus-specific layer on top of this).

## 1. What the IAP is

The IAP is the supervised, documented work-experience program every architecture intern in Canada
must complete before sitting the licensing exam and becoming a Registered Architect. It exists so that
regulators (the 12 provincial/territorial associations, collectively ROAC) can verify an intern has
actually practiced — not just studied — the range of skills a licensed architect needs.

Three pillars feed into registration/licensure, of which the IAP covers the middle one:
1. **Education** — CACB-accredited degree or certified equivalent
2. **Architectural Experience** — the IAP, documented via the CERB (this skill's focus)
3. **Examination** — ExAC or NCARB's ARE

An "Intern" in IAP terminology = Intern Architect / Intern of Manitoba / Stagiaire en architecture
(Québec) — all the same role under different provincial names.

## 2. The two roles every intern needs

### Supervising Architect
- A licensed architect **within the intern's own firm/employer** who directs the intern's work daily.
- Must be registered/licensed in the jurisdiction where the experience is being gained.
- Certifies (signs/dates) every CERB submission — they're vouching for the accuracy and quality of the
  logged experience.
- Changes whenever the intern changes employer; a new Letter of Confirmation is required from the new
  Supervising Architect.

### Mentor
- An architect (or retired architect) who is **explicitly NOT employed at the intern's workplace** —
  independent of the day-to-day chain of supervision.
- Required in every ROAC jurisdiction **except Québec**.
- Minimum contact: must meet with the intern before each CERB submission (i.e., every ~900–1,000
  hours, or at each change of employment). More frequent contact is explicitly recommended as best
  practice, not just the legal floor.
- Changing mentors requires notifying the jurisdiction and a new Letter of Confirmation from the
  incoming mentor.

## 3. Hour requirements (standard / non-syllabus track)

- **Minimum total: 3,720 hours** (~2 years) of supervised architectural experience.
- Distributed across 17 experience areas in 3 categories — see `experience-categories.md` for the full
  breakdown with per-category minimums.
- Categories A+B+C minimums sum to 2,960 hours; the remaining 760 hours are flexible/"additional" and
  can be earned in any of the 17 areas.
- Up to 760 hours of **pre-graduation** experience (as a Recognized Student) can count toward this
  total.
- Minimum time to complete: 1.5 calendar years (even with overtime); registration applications can't be
  submitted before 2 years post-admission as an Intern regardless of hours banked.

## 4. The submission cycle

This is the operational rhythm an intern lives by:

1. Work and log hours in the CERB continuously.
2. Every **900–1,000 hours** (~6 months) OR at **every change of employment**, stop and prepare a CERB
   submission section.
3. Get it certified:
   - Supervising Architect completes Comments & Declaration, signs, dates.
   - Mentor completes Mentor Declaration, signs, dates (not required for all Recognized Students —
     check jurisdiction-specific rules).
4. Submit to the ROAC jurisdiction **within 8 weeks** of the date of the last entry in that section.
5. Keep a copy for personal records — always retain copies of every submission.
6. The jurisdiction reviews, returns a **Periodic Assessment Form** summarizing hours-to-date vs.
   hours-remaining per category, plus any comments/constructive advice.

### Late submission
- Submitted >12 months after the last entry date triggers a **special review**, possibly additional
  fees and an interview.
- Some jurisdictions may simply **not accept** late submissions at all — this is jurisdiction-specific
  (Appendix B in the source manual, not included in this skill — flag to the user to check with their
  own ROAC jurisdiction directly, e.g. AAA, OAA, AIBC, etc.).

## 5. Changing employment

If an intern changes jobs mid-program:
- Notify the ROAC jurisdiction of the new employer.
- Identify the new Supervising Architect.
- New Supervising Architect provides a Letter of Confirmation.
- Close out (certify + submit) the CERB section under the *previous* Supervising Architect before
  starting fresh.
- Begin a new CERB section under the new employer.

Multiple/concurrent/part-time employment (including independent contractor work) requires a
**separate CERB section per employer**, and pre-approval from the jurisdiction is generally required —
this is not something to assume is automatically fine.

## 6. What counts as eligible experience

- Must be under the **personal supervision and direction** of a licensed architect.
- Can be in a traditional architectural practice, OR in an eligible non-traditional setting: government
  agency, crown corporation, institution, bank, engineering office, developer, or corporation — as long
  as that entity has a department/office doing architectural design/construction as an "owner," and the
  certifying architect is employed in that same entity. Pre-approval from the jurisdiction is required.
- **International experience** (outside Canada) must be pre-approved, and the supervising architect's
  licensing status must be documented/proven. Not all jurisdictions recognize foreign experience the
  same way.
- **Observer / parallel documents**: credit for experience gained merely observing (rather than doing)
  is only granted in exceptional circumstances, and the Supervising Architect must flag this explicitly
  in their CERB comments. Don't assume observer time counts at face value.
- Experience only counts if gained **while enrolled** as a Recognized Student or Intern in the IAP —
  nothing gained outside that enrollment window is creditable, even if it was genuinely architectural
  work.

## 7. Fulfilment standard — the part people get wrong

The manual is explicit: **the Intern must demonstrate competence in each category, not merely log
hours.** A reviewer is assessing whether the intern actually understood and could perform the work, not
checking a box that says "760 hours appeared in Construction Documents." This is why narrative
descriptions of the intern's actual role on each project matter so much (see
`writing-good-entries.md`).

Once experience in a given area is *accepted*, it does not need to be repeated (subject to
jurisdiction-specific rules).

"Variety" is also a named requirement: experience must span a meaningful range of project types/scales,
not endless repetition of one task on one building type. Non-compliance with the variety requirement
is treated leniently only in exceptional circumstances.

## 8. Where to point people for jurisdiction-specific rules

This skill (and the source manual's Appendix B) intentionally does NOT contain jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction
rule variations — the manual you were given had Appendix B blank/unpopulated. Whenever a rule might
plausibly vary by province (acceptance of ARE vs ExAC, mentor exceptions, part-time pre-approval,
late-submission tolerance, recognized mentor lists, etc.), say so explicitly and point the person to their
own ROAC jurisdiction. See `references/jurisdiction-contacts.md` for the contact list from the manual's
Appendix D.
